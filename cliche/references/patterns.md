# Universal pattern catalog

Copyright © 2026 Luděk Sušický

English examples; the same patterns apply in any language. Czech manifestations
are listed in czech-lexicon.md. Quote the pattern name in Detect findings.

## Mechanical cuts (always remove)

- **Throat-clearing openers.** "Here's the thing:", "Let me be clear:",
  "The uncomfortable truth is", "In today's fast-paced world",
  "In a world where", "Let's dive in". Delete the opener and start with the
  point.
- **Faux-insight setups.** "The part most people skip", "What nobody tells
  you", "Here's what most people get wrong". Make the claim stand alone.
- **Emphasis crutches.** "Let that sink in", "Full stop", "Make no mistake",
  "Plot twist:". Delete; the point either stands or does not.
- **Editorializing asides.** "It's worth noting", "It is important to
  mention", "The key point is", redundant "in other words". Delete the aside
  or replace it with support.
- **Conversational artifacts.** "I hope this helps!", "Certainly!", "I hope
  this message finds you well" outside a real letter opening, and technical
  residue such as `contentReference[oaicite:1]` or `turn0search0`. Immediate
  proof of unchecked AI output; remove completely.
- **Summary-recap endings.** "In conclusion", "Overall", a final paragraph
  restating the piece. End on the last concrete point, takeaway or next
  action.
- **Fake-profound kickers.** A final cute aphorism or mic-drop metaphor.
  Delete it; do not write a better one. End on the clearest concrete
  sentence already in the draft.

## Structural rewrites (break the template)

- **Binary contrasts and negative parallelism.** "It's not just X, it's Y",
  "The question isn't X, it's Y", "Not because X. Because Y." Cut the denial
  of X and state Y directly: "The eval matters more than the model."
- **Negative listings and dramatic fragmentation.** "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z."
  or "X. And Y. And Z." Merge into one complete sentence.
- **Colon reveals.** A noun phrase, a colon, a dramatic fragment: "The detail
  that makes it work: a separate agent grades it." Write a plain sentence.
  Colons belong to lists, labels and quotes.
- **Rule of threes.** Mechanical triads of adjectives or benefits ("fast,
  clear and effective"). Use one or two precise words, or a real list with
  real content.
- **False ranges.** "From small startups to global enterprises" without a
  difference between the ends. Cut, or give the difference.
- **Trailing -ing clauses.** ", highlighting the team's commitment..."
  State the consequence ("so users can find old drafts without leaving the
  editor") or end the sentence.
- **Synonym cycling.** The agent reviews, the assistant scores, the tool
  suggests: one thing, one name. Repeat the right word.
- **Fake-strong verbs and verbonominal padding.** "Serves as a centralized
  hub for sponsor management" becomes "tracks sponsors, drafts and approvals
  in one place". "Made a decision" is "decided".
- **False agency.** "The data tells us", "the decision emerged", "a
  complaint becomes a fix". Name the actor.
- **Rhetorical setups.** "What if I told you", self-answered question pairs.
  Make the point.

## Vague authority and lazy extremes

- **Weasel attribution.** "Experts agree", "studies show", "many argue",
  "industry reports suggest". Name the source with a date, or cut the claim;
  when the user has no source, ask instead of inventing one.
- **Lazy extremes.** "Always", "never", "everyone", "no one" without a
  boundary. Replace with a number, share or real condition.
- **Importance labels.** "Key", "crucial", "vital", "fascinating",
  "game-changing" attached to things instead of explaining why they matter.
  Describe the mechanism or consequence instead.

## Words to cut

Inflated vocabulary: delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower,
streamline, robust, cutting-edge, paradigm shift, game changer, tapestry,
realm, beacon, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, paramount, transformative,
elevate, embark, supercharge, harness, ever-evolving, seamless, effortlessly.

Empty adverbs when they add nothing: just, literally, simply, actually,
truly, fundamentally, importantly, crucially, inevitably.

Empty phrases: it is worth noting, at the end of the day, when it comes to,
at its core, in today's world, the reality is, in terms of, in order to,
going forward, let's dive in.

Keep an occasional word or phrase when it is part of the writer's
recognizable voice and the sentence still earns its place.

## Formatting slop

- No emoji in headings or at the start of list items (unless the channel
  explicitly requires it).
- No bold sprinkled mid-sentence for fake drama.
- Do not break a two-sentence thought into bullets; lists are for real
  enumerations and steps.
- No headers over one-sentence sections.

## Self-check

1. First and last sentence: no time filler at the start, no recap or
   aphorism at the end.
2. No banned vocabulary and no unmarked placeholder survived.
3. No binary contrast, colon reveal or rule-of-three triad without content.
4. Every claim has a source, a number, a mechanism, or a visible
   placeholder instead of an invention.
5. Sentence lengths vary; active voice throughout; the same thing keeps the
   same name.
6. The voice survived: the draft still sounds like the writer.
